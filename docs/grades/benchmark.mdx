# Benchmark

How well pencilmark's numbers track how long real people take and how hard they find puzzles, compared with other
graders, on a frozen held-out set. Only aggregate numbers are published; no puzzles or per-puzzle data from the sources.

## Summary

- Within each group of comparable puzzles, pencilmark's `difficulty` ranks puzzles by human solve time with a Spearman
  correlation of 0.62 to 0.81 depending on the source, and reaches 70-81% of the best any grader could reach (the noise
  ceiling of each source's own data).
- On the same classic puzzles it ranks closer to human times than the four other graders tested; on variants it is ahead of Rangsk's solver, the one other grader that rates them.
- 99.8% of the 7,623 puzzles get a card; the median card takes 0.15 s (classics 0.03 s) on the machine below.

## Method

- **Frozen set.** 15% of every source, chosen by a hash of the puzzle (its solution where known), fixed before the 1.0 release and used only to measure releases.
  Every fit and design decision since then used the other 85% only.
- **Targets.** Real human data from each source (below). Where a source gives per-player times, the target is the
  puzzle's effect after correcting for each player's skill.
- **Metric.** Spearman rank correlation between a grader's rating and the human target, computed within each group (a
  difficulty tab, a variant family, a board size) and averaged over groups weighted by their size; groups with fewer
  than 15 puzzles are skipped. For sources with real minutes, also the median |log(real / predicted)| of `time`.
- **pencilmark.** Version 1.2.0 (graded at commit 8eb690b), `rate()` with `max_seconds=300`, asking for band, difficulty
  and time; coverage at smaller limits is read from each puzzle's grading time, and the speed of the default card (with
  its technique list) from a random sample of 600 puzzles. [Version 1.1](/pencilmark/1.1/grades/benchmark) has the
  1.0 numbers.
- **Other graders.** Sudoku Explainer (DiufSudoku fork, maximum difficulty), HoDoKu 2.2.0 (score), Rangsk's SudokuSolver
  (hardest technique tier of its logical solve), Andrew Stuart's sudokuwiki.org grader (one puzzle every 30 s; classics
  only on this set). Each is run on the puzzles it supports.
- **Machine.** AMD Ryzen 7 7700X (8 cores / 16 threads, up to 5.5 GHz), 32 GB RAM, Linux; Python 3.14, OpenJDK 17 for the
  Java graders. Grading ran 4 processes at once (one puzzle each). Times are wall-clock on this machine.

## How well the sources agree with themselves

Each puzzle's players split at random into two halves, a skill-adjusted time from each half, and the rank correlation
between the halves (Spearman-Brown corrected, mean of 20 splits). Its square root is the **ceiling**: the best rank
correlation any grader can reach against that source.

| Source | Groups | Reliability | Ceiling | pencilmark | Share of ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| grandgames.net | 22 | 0.94 | 0.97 | 0.769 | 79% |
| sudoku.coach | 2 | 0.96 | 0.98 | 0.774 | 79% |
| fed-sudoku.eu | 3 | 0.98 | 0.99 | 0.697 | 70% |
| sudoku.today | 50 | 0.60 (estimated) | 0.75 | 0.611 | 81% |

sudoku.today publishes only a median of a few solves per puzzle (its reliability is estimated from that sampling error),
so most of its gap is noise. On the other three the human signal is clean and the gap is grader error.

## An outside check: Cracking the Cryptic

Not part of the frozen protocol, and never a fitting target. The Cracking the Cryptic solves in Sakana AI's
[Sudoku-CTC-Reasoning](https://huggingface.co/datasets/SakanaAI/Sudoku-CTC-Reasoning) dataset (MIT) give one expert
solve time per puzzle: Simon Anthony's or Mark Goodliffe's, from the first to the last action of the recorded solve.
All 1,351 puzzles were read with pencilmark's own readers and graded with a 60 s limit; 330 get a card (the rest use
rules pencilmark does not model, such as loops, shading and one-off mechanics, or read with several solutions). For the
284 with a usable solve time (2 minutes to 4 hours, the times of the 1.1 measurement), measured on 2026-11-07 with
1.2.0:

| | Puzzles | Rank correlation with solve time |
|---|---|---|
| `difficulty` | 284 | 0.55 |
| Simon's solves | 116 | 0.54 |
| Mark's solves | 166 | 0.47 |

Median solve minutes by band: Easy 15, Medium 26, Hard 21 (18 puzzles), Very Hard 31, Expert 36, Extreme 42 (5 puzzles).
One solver per puzzle on one day, so the ceiling is unknown and lower than for the sources above. Among the hardest
puzzles (Expert and Extreme) the order is weak: more steps there use computer-style search than the hosts' own solves.

## Coverage and speed

- **Coverage:** 7,608 of 7,623 puzzles get a band (99.8%). Not graded: 8 not unique and 2 without a solution after
  conversion (readings of the sources' files), 1 unreadable; 4 hit the 300 s limit (band Unknown).
- **By time limit** (band, difficulty and time within the limit): 10 s 97.9%, 30 s 99.1%, 60 s 99.5%, 120 s 99.6%,
  300 s 99.8%.

- **Speed** (default card, with the technique list; 600 random puzzles): median 0.15 s, 90th percentile 2.4 s;
  classics median 0.03 s. Sudoku Explainer and HoDoKu
  answer in under a millisecond (they do not model human solving); Rangsk 0.07-0.13 s; the sudokuwiki.org grader,
  median 3.1 s per request.

## Generator

Five requests, 10 puzzles per tool, one process at a time. Seconds per kept puzzle = all attempts' time divided by the
puzzles kept. Where a tool cannot express a request, its own output is filtered and the rejected puzzles' time counts.
The HoDoKu and Sudoku Explainer columns, and pencilmark 1.0's, were measured on the 1.0 release candidate, when "Hard"
meant needing wings or uniqueness; pencilmark 1.3 was measured on 2026-11-08 with today's bands (Hard = the first
learned patterns), and every puzzle it kept meets its request by pencilmark's own grading.

| Request | pencilmark 1.3 | pencilmark 1.0 | HoDoKu 2.2.0 | Sudoku Explainer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a Hard classic | 9.4 s | 18 s | 0.05 s | 0.17 s |
| an X-Wing as the hardest step | 20 s | 66 s | 0.45 s | 1.6 s |
| the same, 180° symmetric with at most 24 givens | 20 s | 64 s | 0.8 s (346 tries filtered) | 2.2 s (440 tries filtered) |
| the hardest level | none within 120 s | none within 120 s | 0.05 s | 0.21 s |
| a Medium killer | 13 s | 65 s | not supported | not supported |

pencilmark is 10 to 200 times slower than the classic-only tools, lands on the requested band (the others' "Hard" is
often an easier band on pencilmark's scale) and is the only one that generates killers and variants. The hardest band
and speed are what the generator's rework is for.

## Run it on your own data

The frozen set stays private (its puzzles and times come from the sources above), but the measurement is public:
`tools/benchmark.py` grades puzzles that carry a human target and prints the accuracy table above for them. Run on
the frozen set's grades it reproduces every number on this page.

```
python tools/benchmark.py grade puzzles.jsonl -o grades.jsonl --jobs 8
python tools/benchmark.py report grades.jsonl
```

One JSON object per line: `puzzle` (anything `pencilmark.rate` reads), `source`, `group` (puzzles that are comparable:
correlations are computed within each group and averaged), `target` (the human measure, higher = harder) and,
optionally, `target_kind` (`"minutes"`, `"log minutes"`, `"seconds"` or `"log seconds"` add the time error) and `key`.
Grading resumes where it stopped.
